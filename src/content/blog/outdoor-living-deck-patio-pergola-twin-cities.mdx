Memorial Day weekend is the unofficial starting gun for outdoor living in Minnesota. After six months of keeping your head down through winter, suddenly the grill is out, the neighbors are in the yard, and you're standing on your back steps wondering what your outdoor space could be.

If you've been putting off that deck, patio, or pergola project, this is the time of year when it's top of mind for a reason. Summer in Minnesota is genuinely short — we get maybe 14 to 16 weeks of reliable outdoor weather — and every one of those weekends matters. The right outdoor structure transforms how you use your home from Memorial Day through Labor Day.

Here's a breakdown of the main options and how to think about which one is right for you.

## Decks: Elevated Living

A deck is the most popular outdoor addition for good reason. It extends your home's living space above grade, connects naturally to interior spaces through sliding or French doors, and can be built to almost any size, shape, and configuration.

### Why a deck might be right for you:
- Your home has a walkout basement or significant grade change — decks handle elevation beautifully
- You want a large gathering space connected directly to your kitchen or living room
- You want built-in features like benches, planters, or an outdoor kitchen
- You want the warmth and character of a wood surface

### What to know about building a deck in Minnesota:
Frost-line footings are non-negotiable here. Your footings need to go below the frost depth (42–48 inches in the Scott County area) or frost heave will destroy the structure over time. Any contractor who suggests otherwise is cutting corners that will cost you later.

Composite decking — Trex, TimberTech, Azek — has become the dominant choice for new decks in our area. The low maintenance is the main draw: no staining, no sealing, just clean it once or twice a year. Premium composites also hold up extremely well to our UV exposure and temperature extremes.

Every deck project is different — size, material choice, site conditions, and features all affect the final number. The best way to get a real figure is a free on-site consultation where we can walk the space and talk through your vision.

## Patios: Ground-Level Simplicity

If your backyard is relatively flat and you want an outdoor space without the complexity of a full elevated structure, a paver or concrete patio is worth serious consideration.

Concrete pavers — EP Henry, Belgard, Unilock — have gotten genuinely beautiful over the past decade. Modern pavers look nothing like the utilitarian slabs of the past. They come in a wide range of colors, textures, and patterns, and can be laid to match your home's architecture.

### Why a patio might be right for you:
- You want low cost relative to deck square footage
- You prefer a more natural connection to the yard at ground level
- You want to incorporate a fire pit, outdoor kitchen, or water feature
- You're looking for design flexibility in shapes and patterns

### The honest tradeoff:
A patio gives you more square footage per dollar than a deck, but the connection to your home's interior is less direct. You're also relying on the grade around your home draining well — poor drainage around a patio leads to water management headaches.

## Pergolas: Structure and Shade

A pergola changes the feel of an outdoor space in a way that's hard to fully appreciate until you're sitting under one. It defines the space, provides partial shade, creates a visual anchor for the yard, and gives you something to hang string lights from — which is its own kind of magic on a July evening.

Pergolas pair well with either decks or patios. They don't provide full rain protection (though adding a polycarbonate or louvered roof panel system gets you closer), but they do cut the intensity of direct afternoon sun significantly.

### Materials to consider:
- **Cedar** — the traditional choice, beautiful, naturally rot-resistant, requires periodic staining
- **Composite** — low maintenance, coordinates well with composite decking
- **Aluminum** — extremely durable, often powder-coated to look like wood, growing in popularity

## Combining All Three

Some of the most satisfying outdoor projects we do involve all three elements working together: an elevated deck off the main living level, stairs down to a lower patio with a fire pit area, and a pergola over the deck or patio to anchor the space and provide shade.

This kind of multi-level outdoor design is especially natural for homes in Prior Lake, Shakopee, and Eden Prairie, where many lots have significant grade changes. What looks like a challenging yard actually lends itself beautifully to multi-level outdoor living.
