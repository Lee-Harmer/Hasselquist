Late June sits right at the sweet spot of a Minnesota summer. The long days are still ahead of you, the outdoor projects are either done or in progress, and somewhere in the back of your mind you're starting to think about what you want to have finished before Labor Day — or before school starts and the year accelerates again.

This is also, quietly, one of the best times to get a home improvement project underway. Contractors have active schedules, but the spring rush has leveled out. Lead times on materials have normalized. And you still have the full back half of summer to get real work done.

Here's how to think about the rest of your season.

## Do Now: Outdoor Projects

If you haven't started your outdoor project, you still have time — but June is the line. A deck or patio started now is done well before Labor Day. Started in July, you're cutting it close. Started in August, you're finishing into September and losing the season you built it for.

The outdoor projects worth moving on immediately:
- **Decks** — permit, footings, framing, and finish. Four to eight weeks depending on scope.
- **Patios** — faster to install than a deck, but material lead times and site prep still matter.
- **Fencing** — summer is fencing season; ground is workable, and installers are in the field.

## Do Now: Projects That Improve Summer Life

Not all summer projects are outdoor. A few indoor improvements actually pay off most when done in summer:

### Basement Finishing
Minnesota summers are made for finished basements. A cool, comfortable lower level becomes a TV room, a kid hangout, a home gym, or a home office — and it's used differently in summer than in winter. If your basement is unfinished or underused, finishing it now means you get to enjoy it for the rest of this season and every one that follows.

Basement finishing is also one of the highest return-on-investment projects for resale. And because it doesn't disrupt the main living areas of your home the way a kitchen or bathroom remodel does, it's a lower-friction project to live through.

### Bathroom Upgrades
Bathrooms get heavy use in summer — kids home from school, guests visiting, more activity in and out of the house. It's also when you notice what's not working. A second bathroom that was fine when it was just morning routines becomes a bottleneck when the whole family is home.

Smaller bathroom projects — a tile refresh, a vanity replacement, new fixtures — can often be turned around in one to two weeks. Full renovations take longer, but the planning and material selection can happen now while you're still in the house and using the space.

## Plan Now, Start in Fall: Major Kitchen Remodels

Kitchen remodeling is a different calculation than most projects. A full kitchen renovation means you're displaced from your primary cooking and gathering space for several weeks. In summer, that's a harder disruption — you're hosting, the kids are home, schedules are less predictable.

The move that works well for most families: **plan the kitchen now, start in September**.

Use the back half of summer to:
- Get a design consultation and nail down the layout
- Select cabinetry, countertops, and appliances (lead times can run four to ten weeks)
- Pull permits and get on a contractor's fall schedule

By the time school starts and life gets more structured again, you're ready to begin. The project runs through fall while the kids are in school and the house has more predictable rhythms. You're done before the holidays.

If a kitchen remodel is in your future, reach out now even if you're not ready to start. The planning conversations are free, and they'll save you a lot of uncertainty later.

## Plan Now, Start in Fall: ADU and Addition Projects

Accessory dwelling units — finished garage apartments, basement suites, detached backyard structures — require permitting, engineering, and real lead time. If this is something you want done next year, the conversations need to start now.

Same goes for any addition: a sunroom, a bump-out, an expanded mudroom or entryway. These are not fast projects, and the planning and approval process takes longer than most homeowners expect. Summer is the right time to start that process so you're not scrambling in spring.

## The Projects Worth Doing at Any Time of Year

A few improvements don't have strong seasonal logic — they're worth doing when you're ready and a contractor is available:

- **Flooring** — hardwood, LVP, tile. Relatively fast to install, high visual impact.
- **Drywall and painting** — repairs, texture work, full room refreshes.
- **Carpentry** — built-ins, trim upgrades, custom shelving, wainscoting.
- **Electrical and plumbing upgrades** — adding circuits, updating panel capacity, replacing aging fixtures.

These can often be fit into a contractor's schedule with shorter lead times and don't require the same seasonal planning as structural or exterior work.
